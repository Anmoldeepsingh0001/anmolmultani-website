How it works

Your home’s value, working for you.

  • Who qualifies: homeowners 55+ (every owner on title), on a primary residence in Canada.
  • How much: typically up to about 55% of the home’s appraised value. The older you are, the more you can access.
  • How you get it: a lump sum, scheduled advances, or a mix.
  • Payments: none required. Interest builds on the balance, and you can usually make payments if you want to.
  • Paying it back: when you sell, move out, or the last borrower passes away. Any equity left over belongs to you or your estate.
  • Existing mortgage: it’s paid off first from the proceeds, which can free up your monthly cash flow on its own.
Is it right for you?

Honest pros and cons.

Often a good fit
  • You want to stay in your home, not downsize.
  • Your income is fixed but your home has grown in value.
  • You’d like to pay off a mortgage or debts and free up monthly cash.
  • You want to help family now, or cover renovations or care costs.
  • You don’t qualify for a line of credit on income alone.
Think it through
  • Rates are usually higher than a regular mortgage.
  • Interest compounds, so the balance grows over time.
  • It reduces the equity left for you or your estate.
  • Paying it off early, especially in the first years, can bring prepayment charges.
  • There are appraisal, legal and setup costs.

Questions

Reverse mortgage FAQ

Do I still own my home?

Yes. You stay on title and keep living there. You remain responsible for property taxes, home insurance and upkeep, the same as now.

Will there be anything left for my family?

Often, yes. When the home is sold, the loan and interest are repaid and any remaining equity goes to you or your estate. How much is left depends on how long the loan runs and what happens to home values. We’ll show you projections before you decide.

Could I end up owing more than the home is worth?

Some reverse mortgage lenders protect against this as long as the loan terms are met, such as keeping up taxes, insurance and maintenance. We’ll walk through the exact protections in your lender’s terms.

Can my spouse stay if something happens to me?

If you’re both on title and both on the loan, the surviving spouse can stay in the home and nothing is due until they sell, move out or pass away.

Is it available on PEI?

Availability depends on the lender, your property type and location. Get in touch and we’ll check your address and home type for you.

What does it cost?

Expect an appraisal, independent legal advice and a lender setup fee, which is often added to the loan. Rates are typically higher than regular mortgages. You’ll get every cost in writing before you commit.
