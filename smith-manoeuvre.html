How it works

How the strategy works.

  • Why it works: in Canada, interest on your home mortgage generally isn’t tax-deductible, but interest on money borrowed to invest usually is.
  • The tool: a readvanceable mortgage, which is a mortgage paired with a home equity line that grows as you pay down principal.
  • Step 1, pay down: each regular payment reduces your mortgage principal, like any mortgage.
  • Step 2, re-borrow to invest: the paid-down amount becomes room on your line of credit, which you borrow and invest in income-producing investments.
  • Step 3, deduct and redirect: the line’s interest is generally tax-deductible, and the tax refund goes against your mortgage to speed up payoff.
For landlords

Rental cash damming.

If you own a rental property with a mortgage that isn’t fully tax-deductible, cash damming is a related cash-flow strategy: rental income pays down that mortgage, while everyday personal expenses are paid with a separate line of credit. Over time, more of your interest can shift onto tax-deductible debt.

  • Who it’s for: landlords whose rental mortgage wasn’t originally used to buy a fully tax-deductible investment.
  • How it works: keep the cash flows separate. Rental income pays the property’s mortgage; a line of credit covers personal spending.
  • Why documentation matters: clean, careful records are essential with the CRA. This is where a certified professional helps.

Rental cash damming has specific documentation requirements. Confirm your situation with your accountant before starting. For planning and discussion only. Not legal, tax, or accounting advice.

Is it right for you?

Honest pros and cons.

Often a good fit
  • You have a mortgage and steady income.
  • You’re comfortable investing for the long term.
  • You’re in a higher tax bracket, so deductions are worth more.
  • You want structure, not a do-it-yourself line of credit.
Think it through
  • You’re borrowing to invest, and investments can lose value.
  • Rising rates increase the cost of the line of credit.
  • It needs careful record-keeping to support deductions.
  • It’s not right for everyone. Talk to your accountant first.

Questions

Smith Manoeuvre™ FAQ

Is the Smith Manoeuvre legal?

Yes. It’s a long-established Canadian strategy. The tax treatment depends on following the rules and documenting how borrowed money is used. Confirm your situation with your accountant.

Do I need a special mortgage?

You need a readvanceable mortgage, where a line of credit grows as you pay down principal. Not every lender offers one. We’ll find one that fits.

What does SMCP mean?

Smith Manoeuvre Certified Professional, a designation for professionals trained in setting up and documenting the strategy properly.

What if markets drop?

Your debt stays the same while investments can fall. That’s the core risk, and why this suits long-term investors who can ride out downturns.
