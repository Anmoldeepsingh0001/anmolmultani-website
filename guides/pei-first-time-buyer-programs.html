Mortgage · PEI · First-Time Buyer

PEI’s first-time buyer programs, explained.

Prince Edward Island backs first-time buyers three separate ways: help with your down payment, help with your closing costs, and a full break on land transfer tax. Here’s how each one actually works.

1. The Down Payment Assistance Program (DPAP)

Run by Finance PEI, the DPAP is a conditionally interest-free loan toward your down payment — commonly described as up to 5% of the purchase price, to a set maximum loan amount, repaid over a multi-year term. It exists specifically for modest-income households buying their first home.

  • Who it’s for — first-time buyers (haven’t owned a home, or haven’t in several years) with household income under the program’s cap
  • Purchase price limit — the home must be priced at or under the program’s current threshold
  • How it’s structured — a second loan behind your mortgage, registered against the property, repaid over the program’s term

Income caps, maximum loan amounts, and purchase-price limits are set by the province and do change. We’ll confirm the current numbers and whether your file qualifies before you count on this.

2. The Closing Cost Support Program

A separate program, also aimed at low- and modest-income first-time buyers, that puts real money toward the costs that show up at your lawyer’s desk — legal fees, title work, inspection, adjustments — rather than your down payment.

  • Who it’s for — first-time buyers (as defined under the Real Property Transfer Tax Act) purchasing a home in PEI, under the program’s household income threshold
  • What it covers — a direct contribution toward your closing costs, on top of anything from the DPAP
  • Why it matters — closing costs are the expense first-time buyers most often underestimate; this program exists to soften exactly that surprise

Want a real number for your purchase? Run it through the closing cost calculator first, then we’ll check what this program could offset.

3. No land transfer tax for first-time buyers

PEI charges a Real Property Transfer Tax of about 1% of the purchase price (or assessed value, whichever is greater) on most home purchases — but it waives that tax entirely for eligible first-time home buyers.

  • Residency test — you’ve lived in PEI for at least 6 consecutive months before closing, or filed a PEI tax return in at least 2 of the last 6 years
  • First-time buyer test — you’ve never owned a home before, and haven’t claimed this exemption previously
  • Residency requirement — you intend to occupy the home as your principal residence
  • Paperwork — a signed Declaration — First-Time Home Buyers form, notarized, filed when your lawyer registers the deed

On a $350,000 home, that’s roughly $3,500 you don’t pay. See your own number with the land transfer tax calculator.

How the three stack together

These aren’t either/or. A first-time buyer who qualifies can use the DPAP toward their down payment, the Closing Cost Support Program toward legal and inspection fees, and pay zero land transfer tax on the same purchase — then layer on the federal FHSA and RRSP Home Buyers’ Plan covered in our First Time Home Buyer Guide. Together, that’s meaningfully more cash staying in your pocket at closing.

Frequently asked questions

Can I combine the Down Payment Assistance Program, the Closing Cost Support Program, and the land transfer tax exemption?+

Yes. They’re three separate programs with their own applications, and eligible buyers can use all three on the same purchase, alongside federal help like the FHSA and RRSP Home Buyers’ Plan.

How much can PEI’s Down Payment Assistance Program give me?+

It’s typically described as an interest-free loan of up to 5% of your purchase price, to a maximum loan amount, repaid over a set term. Income and purchase-price caps apply, and figures are set by the province and can change — we’ll confirm the current amount before you count on it.

What does the Closing Cost Support Program cover?+

A contribution toward the closing costs on your first home purchase in PEI — legal fees, inspection, adjustments and similar costs — for buyers under the program’s household income threshold.

Do I automatically get the PEI land transfer tax exemption as a first-time buyer?+

No — you need to meet the province’s residency and first-time buyer criteria and file a signed declaration form at closing. Your lawyer or notary handles this as part of registering the deed.

Where do I apply for these PEI programs?+

Through the Government of Prince Edward Island and PEI Housing Corporation directly. We can point you to the right application and tell you which ones you’re likely to qualify for based on your file.

Program names, income thresholds, maximum amounts, and eligibility rules are set by the Government of Prince Edward Island and PEI Housing Corporation, and change from time to time. This page is general information, not a guarantee of eligibility — confirm current details directly with PEI Housing Corporation, Finance PEI, or your lawyer/notary before applying.
