How it works

How consolidation works.

  • Use your equity: through a refinance, a second mortgage or a home equity line.
  • Pay off balances: funds go straight to your cards and loans, often through your lawyer.
  • One lower payment: replace several high-interest payments with one mortgage payment.
  • Rebuild: lower utilization can help your credit over time.
Good to know

Do it the right way.

Works best when
  • You have equity and steady income.
  • The debt is high-interest: cards, personal loans, car loans.
  • You have a plan to avoid running balances back up.
  • You put part of the savings back toward the mortgage.
Be careful
  • Stretching short-term debt over 25 years can cost more in total.
  • Breaking your mortgage mid-term may mean a penalty.
  • Running cards back up undoes the benefit.
  • If you have no equity, other options may fit better. Let’s talk.

Questions

Debt consolidation FAQ

Will this hurt my credit?

There’s a credit check up front. Paying off revolving balances often helps your score over time.

What if my credit isn’t great?

There are alternative lenders for bruised credit, usually at higher rates. They can still beat credit-card interest. We’ll show you the numbers.

Is it better than a consolidation loan?

Mortgage rates are usually much lower than unsecured loans, but your home secures the debt. We’ll compare both.

Do I have to break my current mortgage?

Not always. A second mortgage or a home equity line can sometimes avoid a penalty.
